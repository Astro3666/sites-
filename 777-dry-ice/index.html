How it works

Three things happen in a split second

1

Impact

Rice-sized dry ice pellets are fired at the surface with compressed air and knock the top layer of dirt loose.

2

Thermal shock

At −78.5 °C the pellets freeze the contamination so it turns brittle and loses its grip on the surface below.

3

Sublimation

Each pellet flashes into CO₂ gas, expanding hundreds of times over and lifting the dirt away. Only the dirt is left to sweep up.

Questions

Good to know

Is dry ice blasting safe for my car?

Yes. Dry ice is non-abrasive and leaves no water behind, so it is safe on engine components, wiring, rubber and plastic. It is one of the gentlest ways to deep-clean an engine bay or undercarriage.

What is left behind after cleaning?

Only the dirt that came off. The dry ice pellets turn straight into CO₂ gas on impact, so there is no water, sand or chemical to collect afterwards.

Can it be used around food and electrical equipment?

Yes. Dry ice is food-grade CO₂ and does not conduct electricity. That makes it a common choice for kitchens, food production lines and electrical panels.

Do I need to take machinery apart first?

Usually not. Most equipment can be cleaned in place, which is a big part of why dry ice blasting cuts downtime.
